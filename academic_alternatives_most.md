# Academic Alternatives to "Most"

Formal academic alternatives to **"Most"** for IEEE and biomedical journal writing.

| Original | Academic alternative | Best use |
|---|---|---|
| Most | **The majority of** ✅ | General; most recommended |
| Most | **A majority of** | General |
| Most | **The largest proportion of** | Quantitative analysis |
| Most | **The predominant** | Trends or characteristics |
| Most | **Predominantly** | Adverb |
| Most | **Largely** | Adverb |
| Most | **Primarily** | Main contributor |
| Most | **The highest proportion of** | Comparative results |
| Most | **Nearly all** | When appropriate |
| Most | **The bulk of** | Less common in journals |

## Examples

Instead of:

> **Most pixels were classified as background.**

Use:

> **The majority of pixels were classified as background.** ✅

### Additional Examples

| Avoid | Prefer |
|---|---|
| Most patients showed improvement. | **The majority of patients showed improvement.** |
| Most samples were positive. | **The majority of samples were positive.** |
| Most genes were differentially expressed. | **The majority of genes were differentially expressed.** |
| Most participants were female. | **The largest proportion of participants were female.** |
| Most cases occurred in older adults. | **The predominant occurrence was observed among older adults.** |
| Most studies reported similar findings. | **The majority of studies reported similar findings.** |
| Most effects were mediated by inflammation. | **The observed effects were predominantly mediated by inflammation.** |
| Most changes were related to treatment. | **The changes were largely related to treatment.** |
| Most of the observed effects were attributed to oxidative stress. | **The observed effects were primarily attributed to oxidative stress.** |

## Usage Notes

- **The majority of** → safest general replacement for **most**.
- **A majority of** → appropriate when emphasizing that the proportion exceeds 50%.
- **The largest proportion of** → preferred when comparing several groups or categories.
- **The predominant / predominantly** → useful when describing a dominant characteristic, mechanism, or trend.
- **Largely** → useful when something is true to a substantial extent but not necessarily exclusively.
- **Primarily** → appropriate when identifying the main cause, mechanism, contributor, or component.
- **Nearly all** → use only when the proportion is genuinely close to 100%.
- **The bulk of** → grammatically acceptable but generally less preferred in highly formal biomedical writing.

## Recommended Order for Academic Writing

1. **The majority of** — best general-purpose alternative
2. **A majority of** — when emphasizing >50%
3. **The largest proportion of** — quantitative/comparative contexts
4. **Predominantly** — dominant trend or characteristic
5. **Primarily** — main contributor or mechanism
6. **Largely** — substantial but not complete contribution
7. **Nearly all** — very high proportion
8. **The bulk of** — acceptable but less formal/common

> **Important:** Do not replace **"most"** mechanically in every sentence. The best alternative depends on whether you are describing a proportion, dominant trend, primary mechanism, or comparative result.

# Words and Sentence Patterns to Avoid in Research Journal Writing

A concise daily-reference guide for identifying words, phrases, and
sentence patterns that may make academic writing **informal, vague,
promotional, subjective, repetitive, or imprecise**.

> **Important:** Most items below are **not universally forbidden**. Use
> them only when they are accurate and appropriate, and always follow
> the target journal's author guidelines.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Informal Words --- Avoid or Replace

  Avoid / Limit              Prefer
  -------------------------- --------------------------------------
  a lot / lots of            many, numerous, substantial
  get / got                  obtain, obtained, receive, received
  big                        large, substantial, considerable
  huge                       substantial, considerable
  tiny                       minimal, very small
  pretty (meaning "quite")   relatively, fairly, considerably
  really                     omit or use a precise term
  very                       omit or use a more precise adjective
  kind of / sort of          somewhat, relatively
  okay / OK                  acceptable, satisfactory
  stuff                      materials, factors, components
  thing / things             factor, aspect, element, component
  good                       effective, beneficial, appropriate
  bad                        poor, ineffective, adverse
  nice                       appropriate, satisfactory

## 3. Conversational Phrases

  Avoid / Limit     Better Approach
  ----------------- ------------------------------------------------------------
  you know          remove
  of course         state the evidence directly
  needless to say   remove
  basically         state the point directly
  actually          use only when contrast is necessary
  literally         use only when literally true
  anyway            remove or use a logical transition
  well              remove
  kind of           somewhat, approximately
  sort of           somewhat
  and so on         specify the relevant items
  etc.              list necessary items or use only if the journal permits it

## 4. Subjective Words

Avoid unsupported evaluation. Replace subjective claims with
**measurable evidence**.

  Avoid / Limit    Prefer
  ---------------- -------------------------------------------------------
  amazing          specify the measured improvement
  incredible       specify the observed result
  excellent        high, effective, or give a numerical value
  outstanding      significantly higher, if statistically supported
  fantastic        remove; report the result
  wonderful        remove; report the result
  perfect          exact / complete only if demonstrably true
  impressive       substantial / measurable
  remarkable       notable, if justified
  superior         higher/better according to a stated metric
  dramatic         substantial / statistically significant, if supported
  revolutionary    novel, if novelty is demonstrated
  groundbreaking   novel / first reported, only if verified

## 5. Marketing or Promotional Words

  Avoid / Use Carefully   Prefer
  ----------------------- -------------------------------------------------
  cutting-edge            recent, advanced, or specify the technique
  state-of-the-art        use only with evidence/benchmark comparison
  world-class             remove
  next-generation         specify the technical advancement
  innovative              novel, if novelty is demonstrated
  unique                  distinct / specific, unless truly unique
  best                    highest-performing according to a stated metric
  ultimate                remove
  unbeatable              remove
  leading                 specify ranking/evidence

## 6. Vague Quantifiers

Use exact numbers, percentages, ranges, or clearly defined categories
whenever available.

  Vague        Prefer
  ------------ --------------------------------
  many         give the number or percentage
  several      give the number
  various      name the categories/items
  numerous     give the number
  few          give the number or proportion
  some         specify which/how many
  often        provide frequency if known
  usually      provide frequency or condition
  sometimes    provide frequency or condition
  frequently   provide frequency if available

**Example**

Avoid: \> Many participants showed improvement.

Prefer: \> Improvement was observed in 78 of 100 participants.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 7. Personal Language --- Depends on the Journal

First-person language is **not automatically wrong**. Many journals
allow or encourage clear first-person writing. Check the target journal.

  Use Carefully   Possible Alternative
  --------------- -----------------------------------------
  I               this study / the author, if required
  me              restructure the sentence
  my              the present study / the proposed method
  we              acceptable in many journals
  our             acceptable in many journals
  us              acceptable in many journals

**Active example:** \> We extracted the features using RADWT.

**Passive alternative:** \> The features were extracted using RADWT.

## 9. Absolute Words --- Unless Proven

  Avoid / Use Carefully   Prefer
  ----------------------- -----------------------------------------------------
  always                  generally / under the tested conditions
  never                   was not observed / has not been reported
  all                     specify the population/sample
  every                   specify the population/sample
  completely              substantially / entirely only if proven
  entirely                use only if supported
  absolutely              remove or quantify
  definitely              indicates / supports / demonstrates, as appropriate
  undoubtedly             evidence suggests / results indicate
  impossible              unlikely / not feasible under specified conditions
  guaranteed              expected / associated with
  proven                  demonstrated / supported by evidence

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 10. Hedging Words --- Avoid Excessive Use

Hedging is **necessary** when evidence is uncertain. The problem is
excessive or unsupported hedging.

  Use Carefully   Appropriate Use
  --------------- ---------------------------------------------
  may             possibility supported by evidence
  might           weaker possibility
  could           potential mechanism or outcome
  perhaps         usually replace with evidence-based wording
  probably        use probability data when possible
  likely          use when evidence supports likelihood
  possibly        use when uncertainty is genuine
  apparently      avoid unless the observation is indirect
  seemingly       avoid when a clearer statement is possible

**Good:** \> These findings suggest that the method may improve early
detection.

**Weak:** \> The method perhaps might possibly improve detection.

## 13. Words AI Detectors May Flag Due to Overuse

These words are **valid academic vocabulary**. Do **not** remove a word
merely to satisfy an AI detector. Avoid repetitive, formulaic, or
unnecessary use.

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Often Overused                      More Natural Strategy
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  delve                               examine / investigate / analyze

  leverage                            use / apply

  robust                              specify what is robust: accuracy,
                                      stability, performance

  seamless                            specify the actual
                                      integration/performance

  comprehensive                       specify the scope

  transformative                      describe the actual change

  innovative                          explain the novelty

  pivotal                             important / central, if justified

  moreover                            use only when a transition is
                                      needed

  furthermore                         use only when a transition is
                                      needed

  additionally                        use sparingly

  notably                             use only for a genuinely notable
                                      point

  importantly                         explain why it matters

  consequently                        use only for a genuine cause-effect
                                      relationship

  significantly                       use carefully; distinguish
                                      statistical significance from
                                      general importance

  underscores                         shows / indicates / highlights

  facilitates                         enables / supports, when accurate

  enhances                            improves, with a stated metric when
                                      possible

  demonstrates                        shows / indicates, depending on
                                      evidence strength

  enables                             allows / supports, when accurate
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

> AI detectors are not reliable proof of authorship. Focus on accurate,
> original, evidence-based writing rather than replacing individual
> words solely to reduce an AI score.

## 14. Informal Abbreviations

  Avoid / Check Journal Style   Prefer
  ----------------------------- --------------------------------------------
  OK                            acceptable / satisfactory
  approx.                       approximately
  info                          information
  misc.                         miscellaneous, if necessary
  vs.                           versus, unless journal style permits "vs."
  e.g.                          for example, if the journal prefers words
  i.e.                          that is, if the journal prefers words

Define technical abbreviations at first use unless they are universally
recognized or the journal specifies otherwise.

**Example:** \> Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) was
evaluated...

## 15. Common Grammar Patterns to Avoid

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Pattern                             What to Check
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  Dangling modifiers                  Ensure the subject after the
                                      introductory phrase performs the
                                      action.

  Sentence fragments                  Ensure every sentence is complete.

  Run-on sentences                    Separate independent ideas
                                      correctly.

  Double negatives                    Use one clear negative
                                      construction.

  Slang                               Replace with formal scientific
                                      language.

  Colloquialisms                      Use precise academic wording.

  Idioms                              Replace with literal,
                                      internationally understandable
                                      wording.

  Rhetorical questions                Usually convert them into direct
                                      statements.

  Excessive exclamation marks         Avoid in scientific prose.

  Excessive passive voice             Use active voice when it improves
                                      clarity.

  Unclear pronouns                    Make the noun/reference explicit.

  Ambiguous comparisons               State exactly what is being
                                      compared.

  Unsupported causal language         Do not write "caused" when the
                                      study only shows association.

  Unnecessary nominalization          Prefer a clear verb when possible.

  Excessively long sentences          Split when multiple ideas reduce
                                      readability.
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

### Dangling Modifier Example

Avoid: \> After analyzing the data, significant differences were
observed.

Better: \> After analyzing the data, the researchers observed
significant differences.

Or: \> After the data were analyzed, significant differences were
observed.

## 16. Anti-Pattern List --- Always Check and Remove/Fix

Use this as a final manuscript-cleaning checklist.

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Anti-Pattern                        Action
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  Unsupported claims                  Add evidence/citation or revise the
                                      claim.

  Promotional language                Replace with measurable, objective
                                      language.

  Emotional language                  State the observation objectively.

  Vague quantities                    Add numbers, percentages, ranges,
                                      or categories.

  Unnecessary filler                  Remove it.

  Repeated ideas                      Keep the clearest version.

  Repeated transition words           Vary naturally or remove
                                      unnecessary transitions.

  Unnecessary jargon                  Use simpler precise terminology.

  Undefined abbreviations             Define at first use.

  Contractions                        Expand them unless the journal
                                      allows them.

  Slang and conversational            Replace with formal wording.
  expressions                         

  Unsupported absolute statements     Qualify the claim according to the
                                      evidence.

  Excessive hedging                   Keep only uncertainty that is
                                      scientifically justified.

  Dangling modifiers                  Attach the modifier to the correct
                                      subject.

  Long, overloaded sentences          Divide into clear sentences.

  Direct quotations without need      Paraphrase and cite the source
                                      appropriately.

  Citation without interpretation     Explain why the cited study is
                                      relevant.

  Results mixed with unsupported      Separate observation from
  opinion                             interpretation.

  Causal claims from correlational    Use "associated with" or equivalent
  evidence                            accurate wording.

  AI-like repetitive phrasing         Rewrite naturally based on the
                                      actual evidence and meaning.
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

# Daily Rule to Remember

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Check                               Simple Rule
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  Formality                           Remove slang, contractions, and
                                      conversational wording.

  Precision                           Replace vague words with exact
                                      information.

  Objectivity                         Remove emotional, promotional, and
                                      unsupported subjective claims.

  Evidence                            Do not make absolute claims without
                                      evidence.

  Concision                           Remove filler, redundancy, and
                                      repeated ideas.

  Clarity                             Fix dangling modifiers, long
                                      sentences, and unclear references.

  Voice                               Use active or passive voice
                                      according to what needs emphasis.

  Citations                           Report previous research accurately
                                      and cite the source.

  AI wording                          Do not chase detector scores;
                                      remove repetitive/formulaic wording
                                      and write from evidence.

  Journal rules                       Always check the target journal's
                                      author guidelines.
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Quick Memory Formula

**Formal + Precise + Objective + Evidence-based + Concise + Clear =
Strong Journal Writing**

> **Do not treat this document as a universal banned-word list.**
> Scientific meaning and accuracy come first. A word that is appropriate
> and precise in context should not be removed simply because it appears
> on an "avoid" list.

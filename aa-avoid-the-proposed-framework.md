# Academic Alternative: Avoid “The Proposed Framework”

## Rule

When writing a **literature survey/review**, avoid **“The proposed framework”** when describing a framework developed by **another author**.

The phrase can incorrectly suggest that the framework is **your own proposed method**.

### Preferred alternatives

| Avoid                  | Academic alternative                           | When to use                                  |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| The proposed framework | **The framework**                              | Neutral description                          |
| The proposed framework | **The developed framework**                    | When emphasizing development                 |
| The proposed framework | **The presented framework**                    | When summarizing the authors' presentation   |
| The proposed framework | **The introduced framework**                   | When the framework was newly introduced      |
| The proposed framework | **The described framework**                    | General literature discussion                |
| The proposed framework | **The reported framework**                     | When referring to published work             |
| The proposed framework | **The developed approach**                     | When focusing on methodology                 |
| The proposed framework | **The presented approach**                     | General methodological description           |
| The proposed framework | **The methodology**                            | When discussing the methodological procedure |
| The proposed framework | **The authors' framework**                     | When explicit attribution is useful          |
| The proposed framework | **The framework developed by [Author et al.]** | Clear author attribution                     |
| The proposed framework | **The approach presented by [Author et al.]**  | **Recommended for literature surveys**       |

# IEEE Literature-Survey Examples

## Example 1 — Neutral

❌ **The proposed framework** utilized thermal imaging for rheumatoid arthritis assessment.

✅ **The study** utilized thermal imaging for rheumatoid arthritis assessment.

---

## Example 2 — Author Attribution

❌ **The proposed framework** integrated clinical and imaging features.

✅ **The method developed by [Author et al.]** integrated clinical and imaging features.

---

## Example 3 — Methodology

❌ **The proposed framework** employed machine learning for classification.

✅ **The approach described by [Author et al.]** employed machine learning for classification.

---

## Example 4 — Published Study

❌ **The proposed framework** demonstrated improved classification performance.

✅ **The reported method** demonstrated improved classification performance.

---

## Example 5 — Previous Research

❌ **The proposed framework** used thermal and RGB images to identify rheumatoid arthritis.

✅ **Previous research** used thermal and RGB images to identify rheumatoid arthritis.

---

## Example 6 — Specific Authors

❌ **The proposed framework** extracted temperature-based features from thermal images.

✅ **[Author et al.]** extracted temperature-based features from thermal images.

---

## Example 7 — Comparative Literature

❌ **The proposed framework** achieved higher classification accuracy than conventional methods.

✅ **The reported approach** achieved higher classification accuracy than conventional methods.

---

## Example 8 — Technique-Focused

❌ **The proposed framework** incorporated machine learning and image-processing techniques.

✅ **The described methodology** incorporated machine learning and image-processing techniques.

---

## Example 9 — Study-Focused

❌ **The proposed framework** analyzed thermal patterns associated with inflammation.

✅ **The investigated method** analyzed thermal patterns associated with inflammation.

---

## Example 10 — Literature-Survey Style

❌ **The proposed framework** combined clinical, laboratory, and imaging parameters.

✅ **The reviewed approach** combined clinical, laboratory, and imaging parameters.

---

# Preferred Alternatives

| Context | Preferred Expression |
|---|---|
| General study | **the study** |
| General method | **the method** |
| General methodology | **the methodology** |
| General technique | **the approach** |
| Published method | **the reported method** |
| Published approach | **the reported approach** |
| Describing methodology | **the described approach** |
| Method under investigation | **the investigated method** |
| Author-developed method | **the developed method** |
| Author attribution | **[Author et al.]** |
| Previous research | **previous research** |
| Previous study | **the previous study** |
| Reviewed work | **the reviewed approach** |

## Writing Rule

> In an IEEE literature survey, avoid repeatedly using **“the proposed framework”** when describing methods developed by other researchers.

Prefer terminology that accurately reflects the context:

- **the study** → when referring to the overall research
- **the method** → when referring to a specific technique or method
- **the approach** → when describing the overall methodological strategy
- **the methodology** → when discussing the methodological procedure
- **the reported method** → when emphasizing that the method was published
- **[Author et al.]** → when clear author attribution is preferred
- **the developed method** → when the authors developed a specific method
- **the reported approach** → when discussing an approach presented in previous literature

## Best Choices

| Context                        | Preferred wording                              |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- |
| General literature description | **The framework**                              |
| Other author's methodology     | **The approach presented by [Author et al.]**  |
| Clear attribution              | **The framework developed by [Author et al.]** |
| Published research             | **The reported framework**                     |
| Method-focused discussion      | **The presented approach**                     |
| Your own research              | **The proposed framework**                     |

## Important Distinction

### When discussing another author's work

Use:

> **The approach presented by [Author et al.]...**

> **The framework developed by [Author et al.]...**

> **The reported methodology...**

### When discussing your own work

You may use:

> **The proposed framework...**

> **The proposed methodology...**

> **The proposed approach...**

### Quick Rule

**Literature survey →** avoid **“The proposed framework”** for other authors.

**Your own research →** **“The proposed framework”** is appropriate when you are introducing your own method.
